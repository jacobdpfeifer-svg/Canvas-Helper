### Reading notes — Lorde, "Poetry Is Not a Luxury"

**Core thesis:** For women (especially women of color), poetry is not optional self-indulgence — it is a *vital necessity* for survival and change. Poetry = illumination: naming the nameless, distilling experience into language → idea → action.

**Key moves (page 1):**
- Quality of inner "light" shapes how we live and what change we can make.
- Poetry births thought the way dream births concept — gives form to what is felt but not yet articulated.
- "Dark places" within women hold ancient, hidden reserves of creativity/power (not surface/white/european).
- **European mode:** living as *problem to solve* → rely on ideas ("white fathers") for freedom.
- **Non-european/ancient/black mode:** living as *situation to experience* → cherish feelings as source of true knowledge and lasting action.
- Women can fuse both approaches; poetry is where that fusion happens most closely.
- Poetry names the nameless so it can be *thought*; feelings explored honestly become fortresses for radical ideas ("house of difference").
- Jacob's marks: bracket on women's spirit paragraph; circles on **european** / **free**; **w.i.?** likely = "what is?" or instructor prompt on naming/naming the nameless.

**Key moves (page 2):**
- Resistance beachheads get eroded by socialized fears (childishness, non-universality, self-centeredness, sensuality).
- **Descartes vs Lorde:** "I think therefore I am" (white fathers) vs poet's whisper: **"I feel therefore I can be free."**
- No truly *new* ideas waiting to save us — only old/forgotten ones, new combinations, courage to try heretical actions dreams imply.
- Under profit/linear power/institutional dehumanization, feelings were meant to kneel to thought (as women to men) — but women survived *as poets*.
- If dreaming is a "luxury," we've surrendered the core of our power and the future.
- Ideas must be tested in lived context (Sunday 7 AM, love, war, birth) — poetry makes ideas *felt*.
- Closing quote: Lorde as "Black lesbian mother in interracial marriage" — always offending someone's comfortable prejudices.

**BCOR relevance:** Language as strategic tool; feeling vs rationalist communication; naming/ framing before persuasion; audience and identity; contrasts with purely transactional "business communication."
