### Merton — Learning to Live (reading 1, 2026-08-24)

**Source:** Thomas Merton, *"Learning to Live,"* in *Love and Living*, pp. 3–4, 1979 (essay first published 1969).

**Core argument (printed text):**

1. **Learning to live** means living on one's own — spontaneously, freely — by recognizing what is one's own and being "at home" with oneself. That means learning **who one is** and **what one has to offer** the contemporary world, then making that offering valid.

2. **Purpose of education:** show a person how to define themselves authentically and spontaneously in relation to their world — *not* impose a prefabricated definition of the world or an arbitrary definition of the individual. The world is more real in proportion as people in it can be fully and humanly alive: able to make lucid, conscious use of freedom. Real freedom must consist first in the capacity to **choose their own lives** and find themselves on the deepest level. Superficial freedom (aimless wandering, tasting distractions) is a sham — it evades discovering *who chooses*.

3. **Function of the university (first pass):** help the student discover, recognize, and identify **who it is that chooses**.

4. **Function of the university (Merton's provocative framing):** help individuals **save their souls** and, in doing so, save society — from the hell of meaninglessness, obsession, complex artifice, systematic lying, criminal evasions and neglects, self-destructive futilities. (He notes this description is unconventional and "monastic.")

### Lorde — Poetry Is Not a Luxury (reading 2, 2026-08-24)

**Source:** Audre Lorde, *"Poetry Is Not a Luxury,"* in *Sister Outsider*, pp. 36–39, 1985.

**Core argument (printed text, both pages):**

1. **Poetry as illumination:** The quality of light by which we scrutinize our lives shapes what we live and what we change. Poetry names ideas that are "nameless and formless" until the poem — distilling experience the way dream births concept and feeling births idea.

2. **Scrutiny and power:** As we learn to bear intimate scrutiny and use its products for power in our living, fears that rule our lives and form our silences lose control.

3. **Dark places of possibility:** Within each woman is a "dark place" where true spirit rises — "beautiful and tough as chestnut stanchions" against nightmares of weakness. These places are ancient, hidden, survived through darkness; they hold creativity, power, unexamined emotion. Power is neither white nor surface — it is dark, ancient, deep.

4. **Two modes of living:** European mode treats living as a **problem to be solved**, relying on ideas (what "white fathers" called precious). Non-European mode treats living as a **situation to be experienced and interacted with**, cherishing feelings and hidden sources of power where true knowledge and lasting action come from. Women can fuse these approaches; poetry is where that fusion is closest.

5. **Poetry vs. sterile wordplay:** Poetry as revelation/distillation of experience — not the distorted "sterile word play" white fathers used to cover imagination without insight.

6. **Vital necessity:** For women, poetry is not a luxury but a **vital necessity** — it forms the light within which we predicate hopes and dreams toward survival and change: feeling → language → idea → action. Poetry names the nameless so it can be thought; poems carve horizons from daily rock-experience.

7. **Sanctuary for radical ideas:** Honest exploration of feelings becomes sanctuary, fortress, spawning ground for radical ideas — the "house of difference" necessary for change. Ideas once intolerable become reachable through dreams and poems ("it feels right to me"). We can train ourselves to respect feelings and transpose them into shareable language; where language doesn't exist, poetry fashions it. Poetry is the **skeleton architecture** of our lives.

8. **Beachheads and resistance:** Possibility is neither forever nor instant; sustaining belief is hard. We build beachheads of resistance only to face assault by socialized fears or withdrawn approval — accusations of childishness, non-universality, self-centeredness, sensuality. Key question: *Am I altering your aura, your ideas, your dreams — or merely moving you to temporary reactive action?*

9. **Descartes vs. Lorde:** White fathers: *I think therefore I am.* Black mothers in each of us — the poet — whisper: *I feel therefore I can be free.* Poetry coins language for that revolutionary awareness; action in the now is also always necessary (children must be nourished with "real food" for dreams to differ from ours).

10. **No new ideas — new combinations:** We drug ourselves with dreams of new ideas; the head alone won't save us. No new ideas wait to save us as women, as human — only old forgotten ones, new combinations, extrapolations, recognitions from within, plus courage to try heretical actions. Poetry hints at possibility made real; poems formulate implications of what we feel and dare make real.

11. **Feelings under profit structures:** Within structures defined by profit, linear power, institutional dehumanization, feelings were not meant to survive — kept as adjuncts, meant to kneel to thought as we were meant to kneel to men. Women have survived **as poets**. No new pains — we have hidden them where we hid our power; dreams point the way to freedom.

12. **Closing:** If what we need to dream is a luxury, we have given up the core — the fountain — of our power, our womanness, the future of our worlds. No new ideas — only new ways of making them felt, examining what ideas really mean on Sunday morning at 7 AM, after brunch, during wild love, making war, giving birth.

**Attribution quote (bottom of handout):** Lorde elsewhere in *Sister Outsider*: *"As a Black lesbian mother in an interracial marriage, there was usually some part of me guaranteed to offend everybody's comfortable prejudices of who I should be."*

**Jacob's margin notes:** (none visible on these pages — printed text only)

**Agent links:** Pairs with Merton for Thought Project #1 — Merton asks *who chooses* and whether the university can guarantee authentic self-discovery; Lorde answers that survival requires naming feelings, not only ideas, and that poetry (broadly: language that makes inner experience shareable) is infrastructure for change. Strong IBE lens: engineering culture often privileges "problem-solving" and rational mastery; Lorde's critique of living-as-problem-to-be-solved vs. living-as-experience parallels Merton's critique of superficial freedom vs. deep self-knowledge.
