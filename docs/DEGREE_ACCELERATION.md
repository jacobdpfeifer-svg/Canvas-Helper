# Degree acceleration research — outside/online credit vs. CU Boulder IBE rules

**Not an official degree audit or advising decision.** This is manually-researched reference
material (catalog + policy citations, dated 2026-08-31, 2026-2027 catalog year) to prep Jacob for
a real conversation with his **IBE / Leeds academic advisor**. Run an actual "degree audit" in
[Buff Portal](https://buffportal.colorado.edu/) before acting on anything below — Buff Portal is
the only source that reflects Jacob's real, posted credit. Cross-reference against
[`JACOB.md`](../JACOB.md)'s "Transfer credit map" (also explicitly *not* an audit).

Context: Jacob is in the **inaugural Fall 2026 cohort** of CU Boulder's new
**BS in Integrated Business and Engineering (IBE)** — a joint Leeds School of Business /
College of Engineering & Applied Science (CEAS) program. Three plan-of-study courses are
still marked "to be developed" in the catalog (see below), so parts of this plan are
provisional until the program matures.

## Bottom line

For *this specific program*, "load up on outside low-oversight online classes" has a **much
lower ceiling** than it would for a generic gen-ed-heavy major, because:

1. Most of the 120 credit hours are **named, CU-specific, in-person/project courses**
   (business core, area of emphasis, engineering GEEN sequence) that transfer credit
   structurally cannot replace.
2. Both schools enforce a **residency floor** — a minimum number of credit hours that must be
   earned *at* CU Boulder, not just transferred in — so extra outside credit reduces course
   *load* per term, not necessarily the *number of terms* Jacob must be enrolled.
3. The one gen-ed category that outside credit is explicitly barred from (**Diversity**) is
   exactly the category people most want to test out of.

The realistic, high-ROI move is a short, specific list (CLEP for one named course, transcript
re-checks, AP score check, and using CU's *own* summer/online sessions — which count as
residency) rather than enrolling broadly in outside online programs.

## 1. CU Boulder-wide floor

- Minimum **120 credit hours**, cumulative GPA ≥ 2.00, to earn any CU Boulder baccalaureate degree.
  ([Academic Offerings](https://catalog.colorado.edu/undergraduate/academic-offerings/))
- Transfer credit only posts with a grade of **C- or better**, from a course at an accredited
  institution, and only after the college/major department validates it applies to *this*
  degree (Admissions accepting a transcript ≠ the major using it).
  ([Transfer of College-Level Credit](https://catalog.colorado.edu/undergraduate/admissions/transfer-college-level-credit/))
- No blanket campus-wide rule blocks a *currently enrolled* student from taking courses
  elsewhere for transfer credit, but both colleges below strongly recommend **pre-approval**
  via Transferology / a preapproval form before paying for or taking an outside course — and
  CEAS requires it outright past a certain point (see §3).
  ([CEAS Transfer Credit Policy](https://www.colorado.edu/engineering-advising/get-your-degree/transfer-students/transfer-credit-policy))

## 3. Residency requirements (the real limiter)

Two different residency rules potentially apply, since IBE is jointly owned by Leeds and CEAS.
**The catalog does not yet publish an IBE-specific residency paragraph** — confirm with an
IBE advisor which of these (or both) governs:

- **Leeds (BSBA) rule:** minimum 45 credit hours in CU Boulder courses on the Boulder campus,
  of which **at least 30 must be business courses completed as a matriculated Leeds student**.
  Credit that was transferred in (even where it maps to a BCOR-equivalent, like Jacob's
  Rollins → BCOR 2202/1025) does **not** count toward this 30, because it wasn't taken while
  matriculated.
  ([BSBA program page](https://catalog.colorado.edu/undergraduate/colleges-schools/business/programs-study/business-administration/business-administration-bachelor-science-bsba/))
- **CEAS rule (updated Fall 2025):** minimum 45 of the required credit hours must be earned via
  CU Boulder coursework, **including at least 39 of the student's final 45 credit hours**.
  Inside that last-45-credit window, a student may use at most **6 credits** of transfer/CLEP
  credit without petitioning; anything beyond 6 credits needs a CEAS petition.
  ([CEAS Academic Expectations & Policies](https://www.colorado.edu/engineering-advising/get-your-degree/academic-expectations-policies))
- CU-sponsored study abroad, and (per the College of Arts & Sciences policy) CU Boulder
  Continuing Education courses, **count as in-residence** — i.e., CU's *own* online/summer
  offerings satisfy residency even though they're online, unlike an outside institution's
  online course. This is the biggest practical lever for "online + low friction" without
  fighting the residency floor.

**Practical read:** because Jacob is at the very start of the program (first semester), he is
*not* inside the CEAS "last 45 credits" window yet, so the 6-credit no-petition cap doesn't
bind him today — this is the best window to bank any additional outside credit (CLEP, AP,
pre-approved transfer) before that window closes later in the program.

## 6. What outside "minimal oversight" providers (StraighterLine, Sophia, Study.com, etc.) buy Jacob here

Short version: **not much, for this program.** These providers issue credit through a
regionally-accredited partner school's transcript, which CU Boulder would then evaluate under
the same general transfer rules above (C- or better, subject-matter fit, department sign-off,
doesn't fill Diversity, doesn't count toward residency). Since IBE's only truly open,
non-residency-locked slots are already mostly covered by Jacob's existing transfer credit and
by the CLEP macroeconomics option, there's little unclaimed flexible space left for a
StraighterLine-style course to fill. They become more useful only if the advisor conversation
in §7 reveals an unfilled Arts & Humanities or generic elective seat.
