# Reflection — lawn business / accountability

**Genre:** Reflection / narrative  
**Why Jacob:** Concrete work scene; older-entrepreneur identity; hard conversation; lesson tied to end goal, not platitude.

## Excerpt

Work and progress are synonymous, but only when constructive progress is prioritized. I created a business in high school maintaining lawns and providing support for the ever-present need to eradicate weeds. On one occasion I had dispatched a trusted employee to tackle a particularly extensive weed project expecting he would make substantial progress. I was busy tending to the needs of another client and was unaware of the minute effort of my worker. His rate was hourly, and his labor would constitute a payment from the client through me. After many hours and several payments my client startled me with a disgruntled call.

As a young entrepreneur I always ensure quality, accountability, and hustle with my work. Driving my business and my life this way is a passion. My employee, however, did not represent the virtues that I had modeled for him while he shadowed me and received my training. This was expressed by my very unhappy client. From our interactions, my client gleaned that I was new to the business environment and had much to learn. As a business owner himself he remarked that he was impressed with the work I had amassed over the years but that I had made a critical mistake. My employee was not following the standards I outlined for him and was working in slow motion. I now had two problems: a dissatisfied neighbor and client and an employee with a character issue.

I had a conversation with my employee concerning the dialogue between my client and me. Although difficult, my respectful and direct approach caused him to ponder his actions. The next step was to finish the project at a rate competitive to the commercial company who always stalked the streets of the neighborhood. This required me to set aside other projects and sacrifice profits to work many hours on site because of my employee’s lack of accountability. As a result, I adopted methods which hinder my employees from slacking. I learned that when you work you won’t make progress until you prioritize progress as it relates to the end goal. When my employee worked he was not making the progress he needed to until I worked with him, coaching his attitude and character.
