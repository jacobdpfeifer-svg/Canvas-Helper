# Research — UK homelessness / temporary accommodation

**Genre:** Ethics / short research (policy framing)  
**Why Jacob:** Stats + plain-language problem; stacked questions; close that puts people ahead of charts.

## Excerpt (problem framing)

In 2024, 1 in 200 households in the UK were living in temporary emergency accommodations. According to data from 2025 over 131,140 households reported to be in temporary emergency housing. Among the homeless there are over 90,000 children. As we know, England is an established first world country; a place known for class and prestige. Presently, they are overcome with residents littering the street and overcrowding in underserved temporary accommodations. The question becomes, how has England become so riddled with residents seeking shelter? Is it from poor policy, a high influx of immigrants, or a lack of new development? Could it be that there are external factors at play? To truly understand the why and how one should explore all three issues.

As Americans we might know what homelessness looks like. We have seen countless individuals on the street or funneling into shelters. However, the problem in the UK looks different. It derives from a lack of “temporary accommodations.” At its core a temporary accommodation serves to meet a legal obligation to serve individuals experiencing homelessness. Temporary accommodations have to be suitable for individuals which seems fair but many individuals would settle for anything currently.

## Excerpt (conclusion)

Statistics, graphs, and data can only represent so much. What many forget is that behind the chart is a family experiencing the trauma of losing a home. Economic impact is important but people are the fabric of a nation. Londoners are hopeless because their housing situation is hopeless. Social homes are not built fast enough to make up for those being destroyed. Everyone in London is feeling the high rent rates from the lack of basic accommodations which is negatively affecting economic performance. Legal action has not proved to be incredibly effective and many could argue that it has had a negative effect. The UK’s local and national governments are strained monetarily from excessive spending. Like every social problem homelessness won't become better until everyone is unified for change.
