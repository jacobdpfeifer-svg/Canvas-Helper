# Essay hook — Purdue / OxyContin

**Genre:** Ethics / short research  
**Why Jacob:** Scenario-to-wonder-drug setup; rhetorical turn (“What could possibly be the problem?”); evidence → firm moral verdict.

## Excerpt (opening)

In the late 1990s a company called Purdue Pharma began its aggressive marketing campaign for a new prescription drug called OxyContin. The drug was said to be a strong long lasting pain killer that had minimal addictive side effects. It was considered a wonder drug: affordable, long lasting, and non toxic. People who had suffered from chronic pain were able to taste relief easily and experience it longer. Americans loved the drug. As a result, sales exploded and profits were bolstered. The company was riding a massive wave of success. Consumers, CEOs, and sales representatives believed in the product and its result. Purdue acted like evangelists preaching to doctors nationwide. The wonder drug was so popular patients were encouraged to take OxyContin even with mild pain. A pain killer that's more effective than ibuprofen and lasts longer; what could possibly be the problem?

Incredibly, Purdue chose not to mention their drug was highly addictive.

## Excerpt (verdict close)

If the statistics are observed objectively it's clear more people suffer from chronic pain. It would be easy to stop and say that the findings of two studies show Purdue is ethical under the lens of utilitarianism. However, the opioid crisis is real and has had a costly impact. The reality is even if someone took the drug for chronic pain they still would have to face the unfortunate side effect. The negative effects still linger. Purdue’s actions did not positively affect good for the greatest amount of people. Therefore, Purdue was not ethical under utilitarianism.
